# Acme Financial Services: documents (fictional)

Everything here is made up for a software project. Each document says who may read it.

## Travel and Expense Policy
*Who may read it: Analyst and above (level 1)*

Employees may claim travel reimbursement for international conferences with written manager approval. Meal expenses on single-day trips are reimbursable up to the daily cap and need itemised receipts. Client entertainment is reimbursable up to the per-person limit and needs the client name and business purpose. Claims must be submitted within 30 days of travel (demo data).

## Employee Leave Policy
*Who may read it: Analyst and above (level 1)*

Full-time employees receive twenty-four days of paid annual leave each year. Up to ten unused days may be carried forward to the next year. Leave of more than five consecutive days needs manager approval (demo data).

## Information Security Policy
*Who may read it: Analyst and above (level 1)*

Employees must use multi-factor authentication for all company systems. Customer data may not be copied to personal devices or personal cloud storage. Suspected security incidents must be reported to the security team within one hour (demo data).

## Code of Conduct and Whistleblower Policy
*Who may read it: Analyst and above (level 1)*

Employees must declare any conflict of interest to their manager before it affects a business decision. Gifts from clients worth more than 5000 rupees must be declined or reported to compliance. Concerns about misconduct can be reported anonymously through the ethics hotline, and retaliation against a person who reports in good faith is prohibited (demo data).

## Remote and Hybrid Work Policy
*Who may read it: Analyst and above (level 1)*

Employees may work remotely up to three days a week with manager agreement. Work from outside the country needs written HR and security approval and is limited to thirty days a year. Company laptops must use the approved VPN when working remotely (demo data).

## KYC and AML Procedures
*Who may read it: Manager and above (level 2)*

Customer onboarding requires identity verification and a sanctions screening before any account is opened. Transactions above the reporting threshold must be flagged for review in the monitoring system. Suspicious activity must be escalated to the compliance manager within 24 hours (demo data).

## Performance Review and Promotion Process
*Who may read it: Manager and above (level 2)*

Performance reviews run twice a year, in April and October. Managers rate each employee on a five point scale and submit ratings to the calibration committee. Promotion to the next grade requires two consecutive ratings of four or above and a sponsor from the director level (demo data).

## Vendor Selection and Procurement Policy
*Who may read it: Manager and above (level 2)*

Purchases above five lakh rupees require at least three written quotations. Vendors that handle customer data must pass a security assessment before a contract is signed. Contracts above twenty-five lakh rupees need director approval (demo data).

## Incident Response Runbook
*Who may read it: Manager and above (level 2)*

A security incident is classified as low, medium or high by the on-call security lead within thirty minutes. High severity incidents trigger the crisis call and notification of the chief information security officer. A written post-incident review is due within five working days (demo data).

## Executive Compensation Framework
*Who may read it: Director and above (level 3)*

The executive bonus pool is set annually by the board compensation committee. Executive compensation has a fixed component and a variable component linked to company revenue. Compensation reviews are confidential to directors and partners (demo data).

## Employee Attrition and Turnover Report
*Who may read it: Director and above (level 3)*

Overall employee attrition for the year was 14 percent, down from 17 percent the year before. Attrition was highest in the technology department at 21 percent and lowest in compliance at 6 percent. Exit interviews cite career growth and compensation as the two main reasons for leaving (demo data).

## Enterprise Risk Register
*Who may read it: Director and above (level 3)*

The top three enterprise risks are third party data breach, regulatory change in digital lending, and key person dependency in the payments team. The third party data breach risk is rated high and is owned by the chief information security officer. The register is reviewed every quarter (demo data).

## Project Titan Acquisition Valuation Memo
*Who may read it: Director and above, and only members of the Project Titan deal team (level 3)*

Project Titan valuation memo. The indicative enterprise valuation for Project Titan is 1.2 billion with a range of plus or minus ten percent. This memo is restricted to the Titan deal team (demo data).

## Board Strategy Minutes
*Who may read it: Partner only (level 4)*

The board approved a plan to expand into two new regional markets over the next eighteen months. A decision on a possible acquisition of a payments company was deferred to the next quarter. These minutes are limited to partners and board members (demo data).
